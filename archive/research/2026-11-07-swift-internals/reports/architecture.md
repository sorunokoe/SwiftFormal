# Architecture of the verification pipeline, and modern Swift in the engine (prefix H)

Repo state: `d759be7` (read-only copy at `SwiftFormal-internals`). Prototypes and measurements are in
`SCRATCH/arch/` (`repo/`: release `sfverify`; `syn/`: 600-function synthetic target; `inl/`: call-fan-out target;
`traits/`: package-traits prototype; logs `traits-*.log`, `sms.log`, `real-*.json`).
Machine load was between 14 and 23 throughout. Wall times are the best of 2 or 3 runs. CPU times and solver counts are the primary numbers.

## 1. Summary

The measured bottleneck in Scan is coverage, not proving. On this repo's own engine targets (`SFBV`, `SFSAT`, `SFCheck` with `--build`),
64 to 88 % of functions are `unsupported`. Every decided function needed at most 1 conflict and at most 16,515 clauses (MEASURED).
On a 600-function synthetic target, the built-in timing line reads `compile 1.2 s · parse 0.38 s · verify 0.61 s`, and the verify phase already runs about
7x parallel under `DispatchQueue.concurrentPerform` (5.2 s CPU against 2.2 s wall, MEASURED).
So a new verification IR justified by solver or encoding speed does not pay. That covers abstract-interpretation discharge of traps,
bit-blasting simplification passes, and sharing one encoder with LLVM. What does pay is structure that makes reading more Swift cheap and safe:

- **H1**: decompose `SILParser`/`SILEncoder` by SIL instruction family, keeping one closed `Instruction` enum.
- **H2, H3**: typed trap sites and per-site verdicts, for better counterexample explanation, with no checker change.
- **H5**: harden the IR that already exists (`Instruction`/`SILFunction`) into an integer-SSA, closed, serializable form. It fixes the proof-cache key gap (C4/D4) structurally and is the natural home of S7.
- **H4**: contract summaries at call sites. The encoder inlines every callee at every call site, so CNF grows exponentially with call fan-out (MEASURED 2^k on a synthetic DAG, PROJECTED for real code).

On packaging: a SwiftPM trait that gates swift-syntax for `sfgen` cuts a cold dev build from 92.8 to 31.0 s CPU (MEASURED). It does not move `sfgen` to a nested package, and installers can be left unaffected (**H6**). `-strict-memory-safety` is 5 mechanical sites away from clean once round 2's E0 lands (**H7**).
Incremental SAT with assumption-based LRAT (**H9**) is sound and well understood (LIDRUP, LPAR 2024). It changes the trusted checker, though, and today's solves are near zero conflicts, so it is deferred.

## 3. Design sketches (top items)

**H1, decomposition.** Files in `Sources/SFSIL/`:
- `SILModule.swift`: module scan, link, witnesses, globals.
- `SILTypes.swift`: `TypeContext`, `ValueType`.
- `SILFunctionReader.swift`: signature, blocks, terminators.
- `SILIgnored.swift`: the one reviewed list.
- `SIL{Literals,Builtins,Aggregates,Memory,Arrays,Calls,Enums,Closures,Failure}.swift`: each holds `static func parse<Family>(head:result:rest:types:) throws(SILError) -> Instruction?` and `func encode<Family>(_:state:)`.

The dispatcher is a `switch head` that returns `.unsupported` in `default`, so an unrecognised head can never fall through to "ignored".
In the encoder, `run` keeps the block loop and the terminator code. Per instruction it does
`switch instruction { case .literal, .retype, .builtin: try encodeArithmetic(instruction, &state) … }`, and exhaustiveness forces a new case to be routed.
`BlockState` holds `env`, `cells`, `live`, `blockIndex` and a reference to `RunOutput`. The helpers `trap()`, `undefined()`, `call()` and `slot()` become methods on it.
Order of work:
1. Add the CNF-hash test (red if any variable is renumbered).
2. Move the parser families (pure moves).
3. Introduce `BlockState` with the nested functions turned into methods. Keep the order of every `c.*` call; this is the step that must keep the hash byte-identical.
4. Move the encoder families one commit each.

Update the ARCHITECTURE.md recipe in the same series.

**H5, IR hardening.** Neither a goto-program nor a Boogie layer: the reader stays the only producer, and no semantic lowering is added.
- `ValueID` is `Int32`, assigned at parse time in order of definition. `Edge`, `Instruction` and `BlockParameter` carry IDs; the printed name is kept in a side table for messages.
- `SILFunction` gains `closure`: the sorted callee symbols, the `GlobalTable`s it reads (from `global_value`) and the witness keys it may resolve (from the summaries' `equate`/`hash`/`describe` paths in `SILEncoder.swift:691-800`).
- `func canonicalBytes() -> [UInt8]`. Then `digest(f) = SHA256(canonicalBytes(f) ‖ digests of closure entries)`, memoised per symbol. Recursion is already rejected, so the closure graph is a DAG.
- `ProofCache.key` uses the digest instead of `closureText`. Guard: C4's repro must give different keys.

What this buys compared to Kani's goto-program, SMACK's Boogie or Verus's AIR: those IRs exist because several frontends (C, Rust, LLVM) share one backend, or a VC generator needs assume/assert statements. SwiftFormal has one frontend that counts (H8), and the encoder already produces exactly that assume/assert structure (`trap`, `undefined`, `inputAssumption`).

**H4, summaries.** At `case .apply` (`SILEncoder.swift:836`), before `call()`, the encoder consults an optional `summaries: [String: ProvedContract]` passed by `Verifier`. A `ProvedContract` holds the requires and ensures symbols and the digest of the proved claim (H5).
Encoding:
1. `r = fresh(type)`.
2. `pre = encode(g_requires, args).result`; `trap(-pre, site: .callRequires(id, g))`.
3. `post = encode(g_ensures, args + r)`; assume `live ∧ pre → post` by adding it to the claim's assumption side (as `inputAssumption`), which is sound because the callee proof covers every `args` with `pre`.
4. `out.approximations.insert("\(g) (contract)")`.

Spec functions are themselves inlined, and they are small. Scheduling: split `targets` into dependency levels (callees first) and run `concurrentPerform` per level. Measured parallelism is about 7x per level, so the levels cost little unless the call graph is deep.
`EncodedFunction` and `BV` are not `Sendable` today (VERIFIED by a probe: `BV` is a public struct without the conformance). Only proved contracts cross threads, as values (`symbol` and a digest), so no conformance is needed.

**H6, traits.** The prototype diff is in `SCRATCH/arch/traits/Package.swift`, plus `#if Generator` around SFGenKit and sfgen.
Products cannot be trait-conditional: `Product.library/executable` take no condition (`PackageDescription.swiftinterface:470-471`). So `sfgen` and `SFGenKit` stay listed and, with the trait off, build as stubs. This is why default-on is the variant to take. Consumers who depend on SwiftFormal by URL get default traits, but they do not build sfgen, and round 2 measured that prebuilts already work for them.

## 4. Decisions for the owner

1. **H6 (traits) or B7 (nested package)?** H6 keeps one package and the install path; it costs tools-version 6.1 (the consumer toolchain floor goes from 6.0 to 6.1) and `--disable-default-traits` in the lanes. B7 keeps 6.0 but changes the `sfgen` install path.
2. **H4 changes what a caller's verdict means** when a contract exists (a violated `g_requires` is reported at the call even where `g` would not trap). Accept, or keep inlining as the default and summaries opt-in (`--modular`)?
3. **H8: confirm ADR 0008 option 2** (test-only oracle, frozen) and the TRUST.md correction; or option 3 (archive) if the oracle lane's cost outweighs a check that covers scalars only.
4. **H10 is a trusted-core change** (SFCheck learns goal clauses). Defer until a measured workload needs it (loop invariants at scale, N6/N7 refinement in Scan).
5. **SFVerifyKit/SFGenKit as library products** (ADR 0003 D1: "after one release"). With H6 the swift-syntax cost no longer argues for removal. The remaining argument is the API surface: SFVerifyKit has 110 public declarations whose JSON types are the CLI contract. Recommend demoting both to `package` at 0.2, together with the `FunctionReport` reshaping that ADR 0007 already deferred to 0.2.

## 5. Rejected, with reason

- **A new verification IR between SIL and CNF** (goto-program, Boogie or Crucible style). There is one frontend that matters. Solves are 0 to 4 conflicts and encoding is < 0.3 % of a proof (round 2), so there is nothing to win in speed. A lowering pass would also be a new *trusted* layer (TRUST.md lists the reader and the semantics as trusted), unless checked by an independent encoder. H5 gets the structural benefits (canonical form, cache key, integer SSA) without the layer.
- **Abstract-interpretation pre-pass that discharges trap obligations** (intervals, as in ESBMC `--interval-analysis` or SeaHorn with Crab/IKOS). ESBMC injects its intervals as `assume`s, so the analysis is trusted code; a bug in it gives a false `safe`. CNFBuilder already folds constants and hashes structure (`CNFBuilder.swift:35-47`). The obligations it would remove are the ones the solver closes at 0 conflicts (MEASURED). At `-O` the optimizer has probably already removed the provable `cond_fail`s through its own redundant-overflow-check removal. This was not verified: the sparse checkout holds only `lib/SILOptimizer/Mandatory`.
- **One encoder shared by the SIL and LLVM readers** (ADR 0008 option 1 via an IR). It destroys the independence that `proveEquivalentToSIL` relies on (H8). LLVM's poison semantics would also force a per-value poison bit into the SIL path.
- **Swift concurrency for the per-function Scan** (TaskGroup, `sending`, region isolation). `concurrentPerform` already reaches about 7x on the verify phase (MEASURED: 4.2 s CPU in 0.61 s wall). The engine is synchronous, so `async` would color the whole verifier for no gain. The remaining wall time is compile + prepare/parse (round 2's S2/S3/S6/S7), plus the long-tail order (N8).
- **`~Copyable`/`consuming` CNFBuilder.** `CNFBuilder` is a public `final class` in `prove`'s claim closure (`SFBV/CNFBuilder.swift:4`, ADR 0003), so the change breaks API, and round 2 measured encoding exclusivity at 0.03 % end to end. The solver's ownership work is round 1's E1 and round 2's E0.
- **Typed throws on the public or closure-taking engine API.** ADR 0007's reasoning stands; only H9's narrow reader-internal use has a payoff.
- **Per-trap COI slicing of the CNF.** Round 1 already rejected it: counterexample validation would depend on the slicer. H3 uses the full CNF per site instead.
- **`package` access, `InternalImportsByDefault`, Sendable audit as a project.** The first two are done (ADR 0003/0007). The probe shows `SILModule`, `SILFunction` and `Instruction` are already (implicitly) `Sendable`, which is what S7's shared parse cache needs. `BV`/`EncodedFunction` are not, and nothing crosses threads with them.
